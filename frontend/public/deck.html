The problem

A bill you can’t read is one thing. A bill you can’t check is another.

People receive electricity bills, tenancy notices, loan terms and government letters in a formal second language — and no easy way to know whether the numbers are even right. Translation without verification just makes you fluent in a possibly-wrong number.

Millions
of unmetered customers billed on estimates they can’t contest.
#1
category of consumer complaints to Nigeria’s electricity regulator.

The decision

Two tiers. One honest boundary.

Interpret

Any official document. A plain-language explanation of what it says, what it asks of you, and what to watch for — in the language you actually speak.

Explained — not verified
Verify

Documents with a published, machine-checkable rule. Everything above, plus a deterministic verdict — the math shown line by line, the rule source stamped.

Checks out ✓

Interpretation is universal. Verification is earned — it activates only where a real published rule exists.

How it works

The model reads. The code decides.

01Snap — photograph any document, or upload a file.
02Gemma 4 — classifies the document and extracts the fields, multimodally.
03Confirm — you see and correct what it read, before anything is computed.
04Verify — deterministic code checks the numbers against a static, dated ruleset.
05Explain — the result, put into your language.

Gemma never computes the verdict — so a verdict can never be hallucinated.

Fair access · SDG 10

Comprehension is the deepest form of access.

✓
Three languages, spoken plainly. English, Yorùbá and Pidgin — natural and conversational, native-speaker reviewed, not stiff machine translation.
✓
It works offline. The rulesets are local and static, so verification runs with no network — on a low-end Android, installable as a PWA.
✓
The boundary is the credibility. A plain interpretation is never dressed up as a verdict. The green stamp is reserved for what has actually been checked.
